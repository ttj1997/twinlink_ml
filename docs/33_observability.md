# 33 -- Is the bed moisture observable from gas-side measurements?

Status: design, code and reading committed before the run (commit cc256fa); results added after the
run (numbers only from the artifacts in section 8). Branch `observability` (from `origin/master`
9feec5c). The document number is 33 and the finding number 116
(lab book). **Simulation on placeholders; the plant-like case is "plant-like, placeholder", no plant
statement.** Model evaluation only, no training.

## 1. Question

Befund 112: a GRU soft sensor with measured bed temperature, exhaust temperature and exhaust
humidity (F5) is worse than no moisture feedback. Reading of the project lead (decision paragraph
(3) of Befund 112, a hypothesis to be tested): in quasi-stationary continuous operation exhaust
humidity, exhaust temperature and bed temperature are fixed by the water feed; the bed moisture X
acts only through the water discharged with the product,
Delta Y_out ~ Delta X * m_solid / m_air, for the lab line far below the measurement noise.
Question: how large is the steady-state signal of X in each gas-side measurement against the
existing sensor model, does the analytic expression hold, how does it change at a plant-like
solid-to-air ratio, and are transients distinguishable?

  action/disturbance box is widened to contain the new nominal values (sampling bounds only).
* **Dynamics:** at P3, three draws by the kinetics score s = u(critical moisture) - u(nu_max)

  step, measurement and pair of draws: distance / sigma (sigma at the pair's mean value), (a)

* **Context (not part of the reading):** at every lab point the nominal model's steady shifts for

### 4.2 Analytic expression

Ratio of the simulated slope to the hypothesis' slope -m_s/m_air: `Y_out_balance` 0.902-0.904
(P1-P5) and 0.942/0.946 (P6/P7); `Y_out` 0.917-0.969. **The expression has the right sign and
order but overstates the sensitivity by 5-10 %; it does not agree within the pre-set 5 % at any of
P1-P5** (P6/P7 within 5.8 % and 5.4 %). The cause is the dry-air flow: with the density term the
linearised slope agrees to 0.1-0.3 % (ratio 1.001-1.003); the linearised energy-balance slope of
T_bed agrees to 0.1-0.2 % (ratio 1.001-1.002).

## 6. Decisions taken without asking, disclosures

1. **Reading scope (fixed before the run, in the docstring):** T_bed and both humidity variants
   count among the measurements of the reading; `T_bed_x6` (the plant's redundancy) counts; O1 needs
   SNR < 1 at every point, so O2 is "at least one point". The result depends on this: **O2 holds
   only through `T_bed_x6`**; with one sensor per quantity, every SNR of the lab line is below 1.
2. **Two exhaust humidities.** The model's diagnostic `outlet_humidity_kg_per_kg` is the damped
   Picard iterate of `thermo.evaporation_rate_kg_per_s` after 5 iterations; it does not close the
   water balance exactly (found while writing the tests: 0.11 % of the value at a probe point,
   T_in 90 degC, nominal draw). The evaporation the balances use is that of the last iterate. Both
   are reported; the SNR differs by at most 0.02 (lab). Existing module not changed (no fix
   proposed here; the gap is below the sensor's relative noise of 0.5 %).
3. **Density effect seen before the run:** the test against the analytic expression first failed
   at the probe point (ratio about 0.91); the density term was added to the linearised expression
   before the commit. The 5 % report tolerance was set before that probe and kept.
4. **Plant-like scaling knob:** spray rate and solid fraction together at constant water feed
   (rather than geometry or gas velocity), so that air, water load, particle size and every model
   parameter stay unchanged; the colder extra solid enters the energy balance through the
   suspension term (cp_suspension unchanged). Product flows read as dry solid.
5. **Steps instantaneous through the rate limits** of the action box (inlet temperature 0.5 K/s,
   spray rate 0.02 kg/h/s); the 10 s sensor time constant is not applied in the dynamics (hours).
6. Sigma per single sample; averaging over time is not credited (section 7).

## 7. Caveats

* SNR is a per-sample figure against sensor noise only. In steady state averaging lowers the noise,
  but **unknown input offsets** (section 4.4) shift the measurements by more than the signal and
  are not removed by averaging; a steady-state soft sensor needs the inputs (spray rate, solid
  fraction, inlet temperature) known to better than the "observed" amplitudes, or an observer that
  estimates them.
* The 20 draws span X differences of 0.08-0.16 kg/kg per point, far more than 0.01; the fit is
  linear (R^2 >= 0.999), so the SNR scales linearly with Delta X.
* Sensor model as in the repo (placeholder): relative noise 0.005, absolute temperature noise 0.2 K,
  independent between redundant sensors (no common calibration error).
* Exhaust temperature equals bed temperature in the model (no duct loss); `T_out` and `T_bed` are
  the same quantity with independent noise.
* Plant-like: placeholder flows; geometry, holdup and heat loss of the lab line; only the ratio is
  scaled. The plant's water load, gas velocity and temperatures are not represented.
* Dynamics at one point (P3) with three draws whose kinetics differ widely (Delta X in steady state
  0.04-0.11 kg/kg); transient separability for Delta X = 0.01 is not shown.

## 9. Reading

**O2, qualifier "O2 only through the six-sensor redundancy".** At the lab line the bed temperature
with six redundant sensors reaches SNR 1.07-1.26 at P1-P5 (0.68-0.74 at P6/P7); every
single-sensor measurement stays below 1 (0.28-0.60). By the pre-registered text: observability is
given; the failure of F5 lies in training or architecture; next step would be a model-based
observer (docs/12 section I, item 19). Reported with it, without reading: the hypothesis is right
in mechanism and sign but its expression overstates the exhaust-humidity signal by 5-10 % (density
of the dry-air flow), and "far below the measurement noise" is too strong (single sensors 0.3-0.6
sigma per 0.01 kg/kg); at the plant-like placeholder ratio 1.0 t/h every single sensor exceeds 1;
transients separate the kinetic extremes for hours; unknown input offsets of the "observed" size
shift the measurements by up to 3.9 times the signal.
